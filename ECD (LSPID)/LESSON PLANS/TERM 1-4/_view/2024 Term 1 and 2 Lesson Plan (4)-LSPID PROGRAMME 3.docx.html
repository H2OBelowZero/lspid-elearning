Theme: Me and my environment Topic: Me and my home

Start date: January 2024 Date completed: June 2024

Note: Be mindful that learners come from different homes and different cultural backgrounds. All these homes and households should be acknowledged, appreciated, and given the same value and respect during the lesson. The language, both verbal and nonverbal used when discussing these different homes should be handled with positivity, respect and appreciation.

The LSPID team members, together with the special care centre staff, are responsible for adapting lesson plan activities, LTSM and other resources to suit the cultural environment of the area the special care centre is located in.

Throughout the day, observe the learner's reaction to the different activities and try to find time to record learner’s reactions using an informal observation sheet.

Your centres’ daily programme may not follow the same sequential progression as this lesson plan. There is no need to follow this plan as is. Use the lesson content and follow the daily programme you use in your centre/ school.

The therapists and teachers among the outreach team members will guide and support the caregivers / teachers in planning and implementing the lesson activities. This includes assessing learners and guiding caregivers and teachers on relevant accommodation of learners, should some learners’ impairments impede their participation in the learning programme.

Resources:

Learning and teaching support materials that are mentioned in the lesson plan include, but are not limited, to the ones listed below:

Recyclable material

Cereal boxes, bottle caps, match boxes, toilet rolls, newspaper, magazines, sticks, paper towel rolls

Art material

Wool, string, buttons, pipe cleaners, play dough, paint, glue, wax crayons, ice cream sticks, pieces of off-cut material, paper plates, chalk, tape

Photos or DVDs (DVDs not compulsory)

Media of the outside structure and inside areas of different homes, e.g areas where the cooking, washing, eating, bathing and sleeping takes place.

LSPID teams need to supply resources that are cultural specific to the area that they support, taking into account learners’ different circumstances. .

Assessment

Method of assessment: Informal assessments, observations & carer interviews

Assessment tool: Checklists, observation sheets & lesson plan assessments recording tools (LARTS)

  • Carer mentions learners name, while touching him/ her on their shoulder to attain some level of awareness.
  • If familiar with the area where the learner resides, speak about his/ her home environment, this includes popular areas that may be visited by the learner and his family.

Term 1: Outside my home

Talk to the learner regarding the different features of houses relevant to the learners’ frame of reference, e.g. “How does your home look from the outside? Where is the roof? How do we get inside the house? Let’s count how many windows the house has.”

Discuss the different possible features pertaining to the area around the learners’ homes, e.g garden, neighbouring homes, cars etc.

You can also introduce residential settings that individuals from other contextual settings reside in – for the purpose of exposure only.

Term 2: Inside my home

Discuss the different areas within a residential setting, e.g., area where food is made, where the family sleeps and baths. Be mindful regarding learners’ cultural context, e.g. some may not have separate rooms within the house. Talk to the learner regarding the different features of the specific areas and why/ what certain items are used for, e.g. in the area we bath, there will be soap and a washcloth. In the area where food is made, there might be a stove/ pot.

Gross motor

Round and round the house we go

  • The carer attempts to gain the learner’s attention by means of touching him/ her on their arm/ leg and calling the learner’s name.
  • The carer takes the learner by the hand, physically supporting the learner to walk “around the house”. This can be indoors, within the SCC, or walking outside around the SCC.
  • Verbalize the different visual, auditory and movement input the learner is exposed to, e.g., “the sun is warm on your skin; look there is your friends on the grass; the cars are making a noise”.

Learners who are not mobile, should be escorted in his/ her buggy/ wheelchair.

Jump over the fence

  • The carer uses any of above resources to form a stepladder, placing the items on the floor around 2 rulers (60 cm) apart from each other.
  • Carer first demonstrates how the learners will be moving across the ladder.
  • First demonstrate the movement, then take the learner by his/ her hand and support them to move from the one side of the ladder to the next.
  • Only focus on one movement for a week/ two.
  • Consistently verbalize actions step-by-step.
  • Introduce new movements one at a time, e.g jumping (with 2 feet together) over each part of the ladder, walking, crawling etc.
  • Start movements at a slow pace and work towards a faster pace.

March around the house

  • The carer uses masking tape (or chalk on a cement surface/ stick in the sand) to form a large square on the ground surface.
  • Carer demonstrates how to perform simple, single actions that she will be instructing thereafter. Verbal instructions can include:
  1. “Jump in” “Jump out” “Turn around”
  2. “Step in” “Step out” “Reach up high”
  3. “Bunny hop in” “Bunny hop out”

“Touch your toes”

  • Learners can also be shown how to line-up behind each other, on the straight line. Introduce the game “Follow the leader”, where learners walk on the line, copying the actions the first learner (or carer) makes –singing “follow the leader, leader, leader, follow the leader, leader, leader” etc.

Let’s play house

  • The carer attempts to gain the learner’s attention by touching him/ her on the shoulder or leg and calling the learner’s name.
  • Using a variety of household or selfcare related items, preferably items with a variety of different textures, colours, weight, and sizes.
  • The carer uses one item at a time, and gently exposes the learner’s skin to the item, e.g., use a soft towel to massage their arms, legs, hands, and feet; take a lukewarm sponge and “wash” the learner’s hands and feet; immerge the learner’s hands and feet in the lukewarm water; tickle the learner’s cheek with a soft toothbrush.

Remember to constantly verbalize your actions and the sensation the learner experiences.

Play dough house

  • This activity is best presented with 1/2 learners.
  • Provide the learner with a laminated activity sheet, which includes the outlines of a home.
  • The carer demonstrates how the dough is rolled & manipulated to a softer texture – provide hand over hand support to enable the learner to explore the dough’s texture/ consistency.
  • Learners are shown how to break off a piece of play dough and place it on a designated spot on the activity sheet. Encourage the learner to break off a piece of dough & placing it on the “house”. The learner might not place the bits of play dough accurately. Focus on encouraging exploration of the dough & completing parts of the activity (breaking off dough / placing the dough pieces that was provided by the carer).

My home puzzles

  • This activity can be performed in groups of 2 to 4 learners. Announce to the learners that they will be completing a puzzle of a house (outside of the house in term 1, inside the house in term 2 – see addendum for template examples).
  • Start with the 2-piece puzzles and increase the number of puzzle pieces in relation to the learners’ capability.
  • Learners should initially be provided with the complete template of the house as a background, so the puzzle pieces can be matched and placed on the background.
  • Some learners may also be able to complete the puzzle, without needing the background template, by just placing the puzzle pieces together.

Starting a band

  • The carer attempts to gain the learner’s attention by touching him/ her on the shoulder or leg and calling the learner’s name.
  • Using a variety of household items, e.g., a pot and spoon, the carer demonstrates how to elicit sound. While singing a song, continue “playing” with the pretend “musical instruments”.
  • Provide hand-over-hand support to enable the learner to play with the “instruments”.
  • Verbalize throughout the activity what the learner is experiencing, e.g., “ooh the spoon feels cold in you hand; that’s a loud sound”.

Learner’s whose hands cannot open due to spasticity/ contractures – should be supported to gently hit or roll an item with the side of their hand or another body part.

Let’s build a home

  • The carer gathers 2 to 3 learners’ attention by calling their names. Explain that they will be “building” a home. The carer builds the pretend home in front of the group.
  • Use tables, chairs, and blankets/ sheets/ newspapers to create a “house/ residential structure within learners’ frame of reference” that learners can crawl through or sit in.
  • Small groups of learners can take turns to sit in the “house/ structure”, while the carer facilitates playing with items usually found in the home, e.g. play with tea sets, cooking food or taking care of their baby dolls etc. Thus, demonstrating the correct usage of the household item.

What does not belong?

  • Each of the boxes has a photo stuck on box. The photos depict an area in a home or, e.g. a kitchen/ cooking area, toilet, a bed/ sleeping mattress. Play the game with 3/4 learners.
  • Each learner gets a household item that is used in one of the areas as indicated on a box, e.g., spoon for the kitchen or toothbrush for the washing (bathing) area. The carer demonstrates how an item is matched to the household area by placing the item in its corresponding box.
  • Learners with physical limitations can be encouraged to swipe the item off their tray table (or with hand over hand support) into the box they have identified using gestures or indicating with their eyes.

General Accommodations

Physical limitations

Learners who are positioned in a wheelchair/ buggy should be included in as many parts of the activities as possible. Attempt to include the learner by bringing the activity to the learner in his/ her chair, e.g., on their tray table or by pushing the learner to an area where he/ she can access needed items.

Learners with limited hand function should be supported with hand-over-hand support and/ or by adapting the activity in such a way that participation by means of eye contact, gestures or sounds.

Communication difficulties

Learners have limited speech or who are non-verbal, should be accommodated by means of basic augmentative and alternative communication, namely: photo cards, simple gestures, facial expressions or vocalizations.

Sensory impairments

Learners who experience hearing impairments should be positioned in such a way that they are able to observe the carers lips, facial expressions and gestures. Carers should speak slowly and clearly. If the learner has a side that has better hearing abilities, the learner should be positioned with his better side nearest to the carer.

Learners who experience visual impairments should be positioned near the front of the group. The carer should speak slowly and clearly. Learners should be exposed to alternative sensory strategies, e.g. a tactile daily programme schedule, constant verbal guidance and reassurance, contrasting backgrounds for table-top activities.
