1.INTRODUCTION

This document is the official subject policy guide for Life Skills. Life skills educators should regularly consult this document as well as the National curriculum for Basic Education to ensure that they teach within the guidelines of the Ministry.

1.1 WHAT IS LIFE SKILLS

Life skills deals with the holistic development of the learner throughout life. It equips learners with knowledge, skills and values that assist them to achieve their full physical, intellectual, personal, emotional and social potential. Life skills learning programs includes concepts from CAPS Life skills, physical education and creative arts.

2.5. PREAMBLE

Learners gain knowledge, skills, and value from life skills. The subject encourages students to inquire about and practice life skills that will help them become self-sufficient. It enables them to gain and apply knowledge and skills that are relevant to their own lives.

3.PURPOSE

-The purpose of this subject policy document is to guide the Life skills teachers in the school. It strives to leave scope for each Life skills educator to take initiative, especially in presenting subject content and facilitating learning.

- To ensure that life skills are taught and learned in the schools for all grades, with a focus on the optimal, holistic development of all learners

. - To promote open communication among learners, educators, parents, and the community, and to use knowledge in decision making.

- Preparing learners for life and responsibilities

- Teach learners social skills while also guiding and supporting them to achieve their goals. - Assist learners in developing confidence when communicating with others.

- Improve collaborative skills, find a better way to solve problems

- Understand how to socialize, recognize how to act and behave.

- Promote new knowledge and assists learners in improving their learning

- Encourage learners to live a meaningful life - Guide learners in making informed and responsible decisions

6.HEALTHY AND SAFETY

Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Act of 1993 requires the teacher to comply with the safety regulations when issuing equipment to the learners. The teacher may not leave learners unattended during Life skills instructional time.

7. CURRICULUM PLANNING

7.1. ANNUAL TEACHING PLAN

The annual teaching plan must be developed from the DCAPS curriculum document not from the textbook. The targets dates must be set on the ATP at the beginning of the year. Each grade must have its own Annual teaching plan for Life Skills. The Annual teaching plan should be kept in the preparation file. Completion dates need to be indicated after a topic was covered.

7.2. TEACHING AND LEARNING MATERIAL

The Life skills teacher should strive to accommodate different teaching styles. Life skills teachers should be creative and innovative to produce their own teaching and learning materials.

The Life skills teacher will be responsible for accessing restoring and returning toolkits or other equipment provided by the government, other organizations or purchased by the school. Teachers should use the equipment to enhance learning and make teaching fun.

7.2.1. LESSON PREPARATION

Written lesson preparation is compulsory for each teacher irrespective of experience. When preparing for lesson the learning styles of different learners should be catered for e.g Visual, auditory, kinesthetic, reading and writing. Attention also should be given to learners in need of learning high support, Moderate support and Low support

After lesson delivery the teacher should write critical reflections on the lesson, noting how teaching strategies could be changed to meet the lesson objectives. The lesson should then be filled for future adaptations.

7.2.2 Differentiation

Work will be differentiated in order to cater for the needs and abilities of all learners. All work will aim to stretch learners to their full potential at own pace.

Target setting will be used to break tasks down into smaller, more attainable steps, creating an individual support plan

7.2.3. CLASSROOM DISPLAYS

Pictures and wall charts should be displayed on the walls to make the classroom print-rich and learning interesting. Classroom displays will contribute to a conductive and creative classroom atmosphere and stimulate learners visually. Wall displays can be combined with Life skills corners consisting of books and other life skills related items. Wall displays should be changed regularly if possible.

8.ASSESSMENT

8.1. RECORDING

Assessment is formally recorded during the term with minimum 0f 1 theoretical and 4 practical tasks reported on, excluding physical education and creative arts assessment goals. The following should at least be included in the Teacher’s Assessment or planning and Assessment file:

  • Program of the Assessment for the grade.
  • The tools used for each assessment task e.g. checklist, rubrics, questionnaires, worksheets or video recordings.
  • A mark sheet or record sheet for each assessment task.

8.2. EVIDENCE

All the evidence is put together in a report to deliver a decision for the subject. Reporting must reflect and provide guidance to parents through meaningful descriptive paragraphs on what has been achieved and what are the next expected outcomes.

9. MONITORING

The co-ordinator and departmental heads are responsible for monitoring the standards of learners work and the quality of teaching. Teachers are supported by giving them information about the current developments in the subject and by providing a strategic lead and direction for the subject in the school.

Monitoring tools which can be any instrument a teacher use in their method to assess i.e rubric, checklist, observation sheet, conversion table to convert correct answers, oral, written work or recording.
